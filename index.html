Kinematic scheme (schematic, not to scale)

The dashed line joins the tips of the transverse velocity vectors; its intersection with the centreline is the pivot point (PP) — the station with zero transverse velocity. The scheme shows the condition of the schedule entry selected below.

Model

Model. Rigid-body kinematics in the horizontal plane. With bow transverse velocity vbow, stern vstern, length L and LCG at λL: yaw rate ω = (vbow − vstern)/L (shown in °/min), sway at CG = vstern + λ(vbow − vstern), speed of CG V = √(u² + vCG²) and turn radius R = V/|ω|. The pivot point is the station of zero transverse velocity, PP = L·vbow/(vbow − vstern) from the bow.

Prediction. While the velocities are held constant in the body frame, the manoeuvre is an exact circular arc: ψ(t) = ωt, X(t) = (u/ω)(sin ψ − sin ψ₀) + (vCG/ω)(cos ψ − cos ψ₀), Y(t) = (u/ω)(cos ψ₀ − cos ψ) + (vCG/ω)(sin ψ − sin ψ₀). The turning circle centre is C = P₀ + (1/ω)·R(ψ₀)·(−vCG, u).

Scope. Positive transverse velocity is to port, longitudinal velocity u does not move the PP. This is a kinematic model, not a hydrodynamic one: the inputs are already the result of the forces, not the forces themselves. If the bow and stern transverse velocities are equal there is no yaw and no finite PP can be determined.

Ghost ships. The ghost ships are the positions of the vessel one minute, two minutes … up to the prediction horizon. The yaw rate is given in degrees per minute.

Plan view. The plan can be shown as a full plan (turning circles, turn centres, pivot points, marks of the scheduled changes) or as a clean manoeuvre, where only the track, the vessel every minute and the velocities are left. Every element of the plan can also be switched on and off separately under Layers in the control panel: turning circles, pivot points, pivot points on the ghost ships, velocity vectors and marks of the scheduled changes. The velocities may be drawn on the ghost ships as well: the transverse velocity of the bow (green), of the stern (blue) and the longitudinal velocity of the centre of gravity (white); after a scheduled change the set of velocities changes accordingly. The length of the arrows is common to the whole plan.

Schedule. The manoeuvre may contain changes of the velocity set at any moment: click on the time line or press “+ Add change”, then set bow / stern / longitudinal velocity for that entry. Every change opens a new segment — position and heading stay continuous, so the track is a chain of exact circular arcs and the pivot point jumps to its new station. The moment of a change is entered as minutes : seconds (two separate boxes) and is shown in both units, so no manual conversion is needed; the time line and the plan use the same m:ss notation. Editing resolution is 1 s; changes spaced 5–30 s apart are the physically meaningful range (that is how fast a tug or a thruster actually builds up its pull). Faster changes would need an inertia model, which this tool deliberately does not have.
